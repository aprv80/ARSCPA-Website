Plan before you file

Your legal structure and tax treatment affect what comes next.

Choosing a business structure affects ownership, tax filings, payroll, recordkeeping, and future changes. We help entrepreneurs in Katy, Greater Houston, and nationwide understand the tax and accounting consequences before formation documents are filed.

An LLC is a legal structure created under state law. Its federal tax treatment is a separate decision: depending on its ownership and elections, an LLC may be treated as a disregarded entity, partnership, C corporation, or eligible S corporation.

Frequently asked questions

Business formation questions

Is an LLC the same as an S corporation?

No. An LLC is formed under state law. An S corporation is a federal tax election available only to an eligible domestic entity. An LLC may keep its default tax treatment or elect corporate treatment, depending on its circumstances and eligibility.

What is generally needed to form an LLC?

An LLC generally files a formation document with the appropriate state agency and provides information such as its legal name, registered agent and office, governing authority, purpose, and organizer. Filing requirements, terminology, fees, and registered-office rules vary by state.

Can a foreign owner apply for an EIN without a Social Security number?

The IRS provides Form SS-4 procedures for a responsible party who does not have and is ineligible to obtain an SSN or ITIN. The application method and required information depend on the applicant’s circumstances, so the formation and EIN sequence should be planned carefully.

Can a nonresident alien own an S corporation?

No. IRS eligibility rules prohibit an S corporation from having a nonresident-alien shareholder. Other entity and tax classifications may be available and should be reviewed based on the proposed ownership.

Does every new business need a sales-tax permit?

No. Registration requirements depend on what the business sells and where it operates or has tax obligations. Businesses selling taxable goods or services should review the applicable state and local rules before beginning operations.

What happens after the entity is formed?

The business may still need an EIN, tax registrations, licenses, bookkeeping, payroll, federal and state filings, and procedures for owner contributions, distributions, and reimbursements. Requirements depend on the entity, tax treatment, industry, and activities.

Does a CPA replace a business attorney?

No. We advise on tax classification, reporting, accounting, payroll, and financial considerations. Legal agreements, ownership rights, liability protection, and other legal questions should be addressed with a qualified attorney.
