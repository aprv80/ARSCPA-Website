Records that matter

Build a dependable monthly process.

Useful records commonly include bank and credit-card statements, payroll reports, equipment purchases, loan activity, owner transactions, retirement contributions, and year-end tax documents. Consistent monthly bookkeeping reduces cleanup and gives the practice better information throughout the year.

Good reporting should help answer practical questions: Is overhead changing? Is cash available for taxes? Are owner payments recorded correctly? Is the practice ready for a lender or ownership transition?

Frequently asked questions

Accounting questions from medical professionals

Can you work with an employed physician who also earns independent-contractor income?

Yes. We can help organize W-2 income, independent-contractor activity, estimated taxes, and related business records. The filing approach depends on the facts and how the outside activity is structured.

When should a medical professional consider forming an entity?

That decision depends on ownership, liability, licensing, income, payroll, and long-term plans. We explain the tax and accounting effects and coordinate with legal counsel for legal-structure and ownership questions.

Can equipment purchases affect the practice’s taxes?

Often, yes. The treatment depends on the equipment, purchase date, business use, financing, and current depreciation rules. Purchases should be reviewed before year-end rather than only at tax-filing time.

Do you provide monthly bookkeeping and payroll together?

Yes. Combining those services can keep payroll, owner compensation, bank activity, and financial statements aligned throughout the year.

Can you help a dentist or physician buying into a practice?

We can assist with tax and financial review, cash-flow considerations, and the accounting setup. Attorneys and valuation professionals should address legal documents and specialized valuation work.
